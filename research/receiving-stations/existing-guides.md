## Cross-cutting observations

**Nobody has written the "OpenCPN vs Signal K vs standalone dashboard" comparison.** Every guide picks one downstream consumer and documents only that path. A new guide could own the "what do I actually *look at* the ships with" decision point.

**The three hardware paths are real and distinct, but no single guide presents them side by side:**
1. SDR + software decoder (RTL-SDR/Airspy/HackRF + AIS-catcher) — cheapest, most flexible, most fiddly (gain/PPM/sample rate)
2. Dedicated receiver hardware (Wegmatt dAISy USB/HAT, MarineTraffic-supplied box, SARCNET image) — no RF tuning, higher cost, "just works"
3. Boat electronics you already have (NMEA 2000 AIS receiver/transponder → Signal K → forward via `signalk-n2kais-to-nmea0183`) — zero new hardware if you're already underway with AIS on board, but requires N2K/Signal K literacy

**The feeding step is a solved, generic problem** (docker-shipfeeder proves one box can feed 20+ services from one AIS-catcher instance), but every aggregator's own docs act as if you're only ever going to feed them alone. A new guide should present "feed everyone at once" as the default, not an advanced afterthought.

**Turnkey SD-card images are the emerging best practice on the ADS-B side** (PiAware, adsb.im, ADSBexchange custom image) and are only just arriving on the AIS side (SARCNET Mk3, aiscatcher.org's managed mode / AIS-catcher-control). A new guide has room to be the first to document this pattern well for AIS.

**Expectation-setting is weak almost everywhere except PiAware's "2 hours, $100" line and AIS-catcher docs' "6 steps, no config files" line.** Most AIS guides jump straight into hardware/antenna theory without first telling the reader what a good day looks like (how many vessels, what range, how long setup takes).

## Recommended outline for a new "Run an AIS station" guide

1. **What you'll get** — set expectations up front, PiAware-style: rough cost tiers ($15 RTL-SDR+antenna DIY vs $50-150 dedicated receiver vs $0 if you already have AIS on the boat), setup time ("ships on a map in under 30 minutes"), and a realistic range/vessel-count number for a mediocre vs good antenna position, sourced from the MarineTraffic 20 m→25 nm figure and rtl-sdr.com's 75 km nominal / 200-400 nm anecdotal range reports.
2. **How AIS reception actually works** — two VHF channels (161.975/162.025 MHz), line-of-sight range, Class A vs Class B differences (transmit power/report rate, since that drives the "why do I only see big ships far away but small boats only up close" FAQ), why terrestrial reception has a horizon (curvature, not signal strength) versus satellite AIS.
3. **Choose your hardware path** (present as parallel tiers, not a single "the" path):
   - SDR + AIS-catcher (cheapest/most flexible; note gain/PPM/sample-rate gotchas up front so people don't get burned)
   - Dedicated receiver (dAISy USB/HAT, or an aggregator-supplied box) — for people who want "no RF tuning"
   - Boat electronics you already have (NMEA 2000 → Signal K bridge) — for boaters, not shore stations
4. **Antenna and siting** — height dominates everything (MarineTraffic's 20 m/25 nm), vertical polarization, real VHF marine antenna vs "the dongle's included antenna" (explicit OpenCPN wiki warning), coax choice by run length (RG-58 <15-20 m, RG-213/Aircell5 longer), waterproofing connections, indoor vs outdoor (indoor is the #1 "why is my range terrible" cause).
5. **Software setup** — AIS-catcher as the default modern decoder (retire the AISdeco2/AISMon-era workflow that rtl-sdr.com's top-ranking tutorial still describes), the "6 steps, no config file" quickstart framing, a callout for the turnkey-image path (SARCNET-style) for people who don't want to touch a command line.
6. **Viewing your data** — OpenCPN, Signal K, and AIS-catcher's own built-in dashboard as three legitimate options, contrasted rather than picking one silently.
7. **Feeding the community** — present "feed everyone at once" (docker-shipfeeder's model) as the default rather than a single-aggregator afterthought; list the major aggregators (AISHub, MarineTraffic, VesselFinder, ShipXplorer, aprs.fi, Airframes) with what each wants (NMEA feed vs UDP vs HTTP vs sharing key) in one table; mention AISHub's concrete quality bar (≥10 vessels, ≥90% uptime) as a "here's what 'good' looks like" benchmark applicable industry-wide.
8. **Troubleshooting** — a named-issue table modeled on WorldWideAIS's 9-item table: no signal at all (driver/USB power), few/no vessels (indoor antenna, wrong antenna type), gain too high (front-end overload, "less gain than you think"), PPM/frequency offset uncalibrated, wrong sample rate, SD card corruption from constant logging, power brownouts (ADSBexchange's "get a real power supply" lesson applies identically to AIS Pi setups).
9. **FAQ** (see list below).

## FAQ questions people actually ask (compiled from forum threads, support articles, and guide troubleshooting sections)

- Why do I only see a couple of ships / why is my range so short? → almost always an indoor or stock antenna, or gain misconfiguration; height and a real VHF antenna matter more than the receiver.
- Can I use my TV antenna / the antenna that came with the RTL-SDR dongle? → No — OpenCPN's wiki explicitly warns against it; you need a real marine VHF antenna or a splitter off your existing VHF whip.
- Do I need a license to receive AIS? → Receiving is passive listening, not transmitting, so generally no license is required (transmitting AIS, by contrast, is regulated) — call this out since "do I need a ham license" is a recurring conflation with ADS-B/APRS feeding conventions (aprs.fi's use of ham callsigns as station IDs feeds this confusion).
- What's the difference between Class A and Class B AIS, and does it affect what I can receive? → Class A (large commercial vessels, higher power, faster reporting) is easier to receive at range than Class B (recreational, lower power, slower reporting); explains why distant contacts skew toward big ships.
- Will running an AIS receiver drain my boat's battery? → A Pi + RTL-SDR draws very little power (a few watts) compared to any transmitting equipment; framed correctly this is a non-issue, but worth an explicit reassurance since it's a recurring underlying worry behind "will this affect my house bank" questions on boat forums.
- Why did my SD card die / corrupt after a few months? → Constant logging/writes to a consumer microSD card is the classic Pi failure mode; recommend log rotation, tmpfs for high-write directories, or a "no-SD-writes" image design (ADSBexchange's stated design goal, directly transferable to AIS Pi images).
- My gain is maxed out but I still don't see anything — should I go higher? → No — maximum gain overloads the front end and produces duplicate/garbled signal spikes; the goal is optimum, not maximum, gain (documented in AIS-catcher's own gain-tuning discussion and general RTL-SDR FM-overload guidance).
- Do I need to set a PPM/frequency correction? → Yes, unless your dongle has a TCXO; incorrect PPM is one of the most common "compiles and runs but hears nothing" problems.
- What sample rate should I use? → Guides don't converge on one universal number, but under- or mis-set sample rates relative to the dongle's actual capability is a repeated stated cause of missed decodes — worth a concrete recommended default in the new guide rather than leaving it to trial and error.
- Can I feed more than one site with the same setup? → Yes, and you should — docker-shipfeeder proves a single AIS-catcher instance can fan out to 20+ aggregators simultaneously; there's no reason to pick just one.
- Can I use my boat's existing AIS transponder/receiver instead of buying an SDR? → Yes, via NMEA 2000 → Signal K → `signalk-n2kais-to-nmea0183`, as documented by Signal K's "Roaming AIS Station" post — most relevant to boaters who already have AIS aboard and want to contribute while underway rather than build a fixed shore station.
- How much does this cost and how long does setup take? → No AIS guide states this crisply; borrow PiAware's framing ("~$100, 2 hours") and adapt: an RTL-SDR + antenna DIY path can be under $30-40, a dedicated dAISy-class receiver runs higher, and either can go from box to "ships on a map" in under 30-60 minutes per the AIS-catcher docs' 6-step claim.
